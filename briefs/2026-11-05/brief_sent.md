## Top Line
Permission has become the scarcest input in the AI buildout, and voters, governments and finance chiefs are each rationing it in their own way. Communities in dozens of US ballot measures backed bans, moratoriums or other curbs on data centers, Meta’s president warned the midterm results will make construction harder, and AI infrastructure stocks fell as investors priced the risk. Washington’s reach now extends offshore as well, with Oracle canceling its $7 billion lease of advanced chips to Tencent across Southeast Asian data centers after US officials raised concerns. Inside companies the same discipline is arriving through budgets, as Apple cuts what its engineers may spend on AI each day and Goldman Sachs and Apollo look for outside buyers to share a $40 billion SpaceX chip financing. Bill Gates added the security case, warning that leaving AI safety to industry self-regulation exposes the world to AI-enabled bioweapons and cyberattacks and urging Washington to set safety benchmarks for models.

## Strategic Counter Narrative
Opposition to data centers in the US now has a policy agenda behind it: analysts expect the new Democratic Congress to cut tax incentives and make developers pay for the grid upgrades their sites require, costs that until now have fallen on local customers. The constraint on AI is moving from chips and capital to where a country can secure power, land and public consent at the same time, which helps explain why Google is committing $15 billion to Malaysia, where the government is actively courting the investment. The UAE has built its AI strategy around that alignment, planning energy, compute and public policy together, a link ADIPEC put at the center of its program this week. Public engagement points the same way, with more than 200,000 people taking part in the UAE AI Office’s vibe coding initiative with Microsoft while American communities vote the infrastructure down. Inception42’s agreements with ADNOC and OpenAI, signed on ADIPEC’s opening day, put that model to work inside Abu Dhabi’s largest industry. ADIPEC closes today, with energy and technology companies from around the world working out how to bring AI into their operations at scale.
