Top Line

The US midterms have turned the data center, the physical base of the AI boom, into the industry's most contested piece of infrastructure. Democrats took both chambers of Congress and the Texas governorship, and voters sided against data centers in almost every local ballot measure, with members of the new majority already promising hearings for tech executives. Markets split the AI trade in two: the largest tech companies added close to $1 trillion in value on bets that spending will slow, while data center builders fell sharply and CoreWeave's bonds traded at distressed levels. Supply is tightening from the other direction, as Oracle dropped a $7 billion chip lease with Tencent under US pressure and Chinese developers expect to receive about 1% of the Nvidia chips they ordered. Inside companies the same discipline is already visible, with Apple cutting the daily AI budgets of its own engineers and Google widening voluntary buyouts as it contains costs around its AI spending.

Power

  • Democrats' sweep of Congress positions the new majority to call tech executives to hearings and write AI rules, with Progressive Caucus chair Greg Casar calling the result "the early tremor of a much bigger AI earthquake." The result weakens the long-held assumption that Congress would pass nothing on AI. Politico / The Wall Street Journal
  • Pentagon chief technology officer Emil Michael, vice chair of the Super Intelligence Task Force, said the government will publish the laws and regulations that apply to US AI labs "in the next week or two." He framed it as an alternative to "535 legislators writing a piece of regulation that could kill the industry." Reuters / The Straits Times
  • Oracle has canceled a five-year, $7 billion agreement to lease 100,000 chips to Tencent after US officials raised concerns, the Chinese firm's largest overseas deal. Offshore compute leasing, the main route around export controls, now carries political risk for the US provider as well as the Chinese buyer. Financial Times / Bloomberg
  • Following up Wednesday's brief on Nvidia's 51% rule, Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and others have ordered about 6 million RTX Pro 5500 chips but expect roughly 1% within four months as Nvidia struggles to raise output. Even chips cleared for export are reaching China in token volumes. Bloomberg / Data Center Dynamics

Regulation & Trust

  • Of 41 local data center measures in 10 states, voters sided against the facilities in every one with results available except Harwood, North Dakota, and 56% told a CNBC poll data centers were at least a major factor in their decision. Local permission has become a binding constraint on AI capacity alongside chips and power. CNBC / Semafor / The Washington Post
  • The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued its first guidance on how examiners should probe banks' AI use, telling them to judge the consequences of AI-driven mistakes over model sophistication. A simple agent authorized to initiate transactions may count as riskier than an advanced internal model, putting agent autonomy at the center of bank supervision. Reuters
  • A Sao Paulo court ordered Meta to stop collecting content from outlets in three Brazilian press associations to train its AI, suspending new business terms and setting a fine of one million reais a day. Making free AI training a condition of platform access is now being tested in court. The Straits Times / Semafor

Strategic Counter Narrative

Goldman Sachs economists estimate that Democratic wins at state and local level could bring short-term moratoria affecting about 45% of planned US data center capacity. The limits on AI growth have mostly been measured in chips and power; after Tuesday's vote, permission from voters, legislators and lenders sits beside them. The same pressure runs through supply, where an Oracle lease to Tencent collapsed under US scrutiny and Chinese buyers expect a sliver of the Nvidia chips they ordered. Supervisors are adjusting too, with the US bank regulator now telling examiners to weigh the consequences of an AI mistake ahead of the sophistication of the model. For the Gulf, the shift cuts both ways: abundant energy, land and fast permits become a sharper advantage, while a Democratic Congress is expected to look harder at how US chips are safeguarded once they arrive. Inception42's Ashish Koshy told AGBI that capacity in the UAE should be built alongside the workloads that will use it. As ADIPEC reaches its final day, its sessions have moved from AI experimentation toward enterprise-scale deployment.
