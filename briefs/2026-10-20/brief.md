## Top Line
Enterprises and governments are moving to put their own controls between frontier models and the systems those models touch. Microsoft made the case most bluntly, launching Guardian Agents on the premise that frontier models cannot be trusted and that authority over agents must sit outside the labs that supply them. Washington applied the same logic through procurement, with DHS ordering civilian agencies to strip out DeepSeek and any third-party tool built on its models. Suppliers are adjusting to that demand: Nvidia released open models that companies can post-train and host themselves, while Amazon’s TorchNeuron lowers the cost of moving PyTorch workloads off Nvidia chips. Emirates NBD’s decision to run OpenAI models on in-country infrastructure and etisalat’s externally audited AI certification bring the same instinct to the Gulf, even as Anthropic’s talks for a second $45 billion Nscale contract show how much capacity the labs still need to lock in to serve that demand.

## Strategic Counter Narrative
Microsoft’s Defender can now flag up to 60 kinds of agents that employees bring into the workplace on their own, which means the first governance failure most companies face will come from tools already inside their networks, well before any regulator acts. The industry answer taking shape treats oversight like audit, with monitoring kept independent of whoever supplies the model, and Washington’s DeepSeek order extends that logic to the software supply chain by reaching every tool built on a disallowed model. The UAE is building the same separation into its market from both ends: etisalat had its AI management system certified by an outside assessor against ISO/IEC 42001, and Emirates NBD required that OpenAI’s models answer from infrastructure inside the country. Demand is running ahead of those controls, with one in ten UAE smartphone users already relying on agents and more than half of daily users worried about assistants impersonating them. Deployment partners working with UAE institutions will be asked for the same evidence those two companies now hold: where the model runs, who audited the controls, and who can stop an agent mid-task.
