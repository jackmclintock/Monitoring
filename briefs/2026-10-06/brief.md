## Top Line
AI Everything opens in Abu Dhabi at the moment the question buyers ask of AI has shifted from what it can do to whether it can be controlled. The global evidence is mounting: Wikimedia has linked OpenAI agents to a partial outage of its data service in May, and four frontier labs were questioned under oath by the New York City Council on how they quantify catastrophic risk. The UAE is exposed to that question because its companies run more AI agents than those of any other surveyed market, while only 5 percent of its CIOs say they could shut down a faulty agent within two hours. Dubai's new accelerator, covering more than 300 government use cases, and Core42's partner program built on sovereign cloud controls show the Gulf answering with governance built in from the start. Whoever can show a buyer that an agent can be inventoried, owned and stopped will set the terms for the next round of deployments.

## Strategic Counter Narrative
OpenAI's own tests show its text watermark detection falling from about 92 percent to 66 percent once a tenth of the words are swapped, a reminder that safeguards built into a model have measurable limits. Buyers in regulated sectors are drawing the same conclusion about agents, and will look for supervision at the level of the deployment: an inventory of every agent, a named owner for each, and a tested way to stop it. Rules already in force in the EU and bills proposed in New York ask providers for that kind of evidence. Open-weight releases such as Reflection's Beam, which sovereign customers can run themselves, raise the stakes further, since the buyer then owns both the model and its operational risk. Abu Dhabi is well placed to meet that demand because sovereign cloud, government customers and enterprise integration already sit inside one ecosystem, from Core42 and Presight to Microsoft-based deployments. AI Everything, running October 6 and 7 with policymakers, investors and enterprise buyers in the same halls, is where those supervision questions will be put to vendors directly.
