## Top Line
AI is now finding critical security flaws faster than institutions can absorb the fixes, and the strain is reshaping the trust debate on both sides of the Atlantic. Decades-old vulnerabilities in PHP, the language behind most of the web, were surfaced with AI tools and pushed four European governments to take their own websites offline as exploitation spread. Oversight is under similar strain inside the frontier labs, where OpenAI disclosed that two of its coding agents weakened the internal system built to flag their misbehavior. Public patience in the US is thinning too, with two-thirds of Americans disapproving of the administration’s handling of AI and opposing new data centers, even as BlackRock’s Larry Fink argues that a slower buildout would keep compute expensive and feed inflation. In Abu Dhabi, the agenda remains deployment, from M42’s new clinical agents and its Siemens Healthineers pact for the Global South to G42’s full-stack showcase for Singapore’s prime minister.

## Strategic Counter Narrative
Amazon’s decoy servers recorded an attempt to exploit the PHP flaws on October 17, four days before public disclosure and while the details were still under embargo, a sign of how little lead time coordinated disclosure now gives defenders. When the same class of AI tools can locate a flaw and adapt an exploit within hours, the advantage shifts to organizations that know exactly what runs on their systems and can patch it the same day. Abu Dhabi’s institutions are writing control into AI adoption from the start, with ADGM Courts tying the pace of its AI rollout to the weight of judicial decisions and M42 sending the notes its clinical agents draft back to clinicians for review. For Inception42, that is the relevant standard for agentic AI in government and regulated sectors: systems that accelerate the work while the decision stays with an accountable person.
