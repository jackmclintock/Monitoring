Top Line

The AI build-out is now being financed in public, and the bill is in plain view. Anthropic's amended IPO filing shows nearly $17 billion in quarterly revenue alongside $895 billion of computing commitments and a $23 billion quarterly loss, while investors push its valuation toward $1.5 trillion. Oracle's $20 billion fundraising and Marvell's $115 billion bet on Lumentum met falling share prices, as the Nasdaq 100 slipped into correction and memory chip prices, now double their level at the start of the year, squeezed the rest of the electronics market. The same filing says Anthropic may be close to AI that accelerates its own development, and that it is weighing tighter human oversight of research decisions. In Abu Dhabi, the OpenAI agreements signed with ADNOC, Mubadala and the government drew international coverage as Sam Altman told the ADIPEC opening there must be "no room for debate" over human control of AI.

Strategic Counter Narrative

On November 2, Anthropic told prospective shareholders that it may soon reach its own threshold for AI-accelerated progress and that it is considering tighter human oversight of research decisions. Control is becoming something companies must show, as Goldman Sachs restricts AI coding tools to engineers and the UK's cyber agency expects AI-enabled attacks on infrastructure within two years. In Abu Dhabi, the same principle was stated from the ADIPEC stage, with Sam Altman saying human control of AI leaves no room for debate. The government agreements put it into practice, with each automated compliance check traceable to its governing rule and final decisions left with officials, while Al Olama sets out standards to protect data centers. Inception42's agreements with ADNOC and Mubadala bring that model into energy and investment decisions. As ADIPEC continues, energy producers and technology companies from around the world are weighing how much of their operations to hand to AI, and on what terms.
