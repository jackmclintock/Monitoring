## Top Line
The AI build-out now depends on financing that markets are growing reluctant to provide. As the Nasdaq 100 slipped into correction, Oracle and Alphabet returned to bond investors, and Marvell’s $115 billion bid for Lumentum sent both stocks sharply lower. Anthropic’s IPO filing shows why the borrowing will not stop: the company has committed nearly $900 billion to compute and warns that AI may soon sharply accelerate its own research, a race no frontier lab can afford to sit out. Where private investors hesitate, governments are stepping in: Seoul is nearly doubling its AI and chip budget to build a national frontier model, and OpenAI is deepening its ties with Washington by hiring a former Army secretary. In Abu Dhabi, that state backing is already turning into deployment, with agreements signed at ADIPEC bringing OpenAI’s frontier models, through Inception42, into government services, ADNOC and Mubadala.

## Strategic Counter Narrative
Anthropic’s IPO filing warns that its AI may soon speed up its own development, and the company says it will keep humans in control of that process. Attacks on government systems are raising the same concern: emergency alert apps in Ukraine and Russia were hijacked to send false missile warnings, and an AI agent ran inside a US government network for months before it was detected. Financial regulators are setting rules for AI agents, with Hong Kong’s monetary authority consulting on a know-your-agent standard and Dubai’s financial regulator publishing work on agentic AI and cyber resilience. Abu Dhabi’s agreements with OpenAI include similar safeguards: AI-assisted government assessments must be traceable to the rule behind them, with final decisions left to officials, and at ADNOC, the OpenAI models deployed by Inception42 support operational and commercial decisions that remain with its teams. ADIPEC continues this week, with energy companies from around the world discussing how to use AI in their operations.
