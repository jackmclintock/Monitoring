## Top Line
Over the weekend, the rules around AI hardened on three fronts at once: exports, autonomous agents and the cost of capital. China published draft export controls on 40 technologies, 23 of them tied to AI, while Taiwan plans to make AI chip diversion to China a criminal offense, writing access to compute into law on both sides of the US-China trade truce. Autonomous agents are meeting the same hardening, as India’s cyber agency reported agents from several providers reaching live corporate systems and Citi barred third-party agents from customer accounts. Capital is repricing the buildout too, with CoreWeave’s bonds trading near distress levels after a sharp selloff and Anthropic offering Blackstone a double-digit yield for chip financing. Any organization deploying AI at scale now has to plan around where models and chips may travel, what agents may touch and what compute costs to finance.

## Strategic Counter Narrative
Microsoft’s reported exit from its Nscale deal in the UK, after grid-connection delays at the Essex campus and OpenAI’s pause of Stargate UK over energy costs, shows that power availability and permitting now decide where AI capacity gets built as much as capital does. The same logic runs through Accenture’s planned sale of its Greater China unit, where security rules have forced separate IT systems, and through Malaysia’s six locally designed chips, a bid to capture more of the value chain than packaging alone offers. For the Gulf, the binding constraint is the pace of US chip deliveries, which investment negotiations, infrastructure limits and Washington’s leakage concerns are slowing, with Nvidia’s policy lead warning that delays could raise the appeal of Chinese alternatives. The UAE is building around what it controls: Core42’s Sovereign Public Cloud is the onboarding route for Microsoft’s MDASH across government entities, and the TDRA 6G Summit opening on October 19 puts the next network generation on the national agenda. Gulf platforms that already combine sovereign hosting with governed, tested deployment hold the stronger hand when location, security rules and supply chains decide who can run what.
