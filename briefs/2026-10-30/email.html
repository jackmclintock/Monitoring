Good morning team,

Top Line

Alphabet's record $500 billion one-day loss confirmed that markets now judge AI builders by who can fund the build-out, and Amazon's results pushed that verdict further. Amazon burned $42.4 billion over twelve months as quarterly capital spending hit $77.26 billion, even with AWS growing at its fastest pace in years. Microsoft, the one hyperscaler still generating cash, turned to the bond market for the first time since 2017, while lenders such as HSBC look for ways to share data center risk. Governments are stepping in where private capital hesitates, with Ottawa taking close to a quarter of Cohere's new round and Mistral releasing its trillion-parameter model as weights that cannot be switched off. Over all of it hangs a New York Times account of a suspected Chinese intelligence asset close to Jensen Huang, which puts Nvidia's role in export policy back under scrutiny.

Power

  • A New York Times investigation reported that US agencies concluded an executive at Singapore's Megaspeed, suspected of steering Nvidia chips to China, was a Chinese intelligence asset close to Jensen Huang, and that President Trump took no action after a briefing. Huang called the dealings "normal," but the report puts Nvidia's influence over chip export policy under new scrutiny. The New York Times / Bloomberg / CNBC
  • Cohere completed the first close of a round expected to reach about $5 billion at a $20.5 billion valuation, with the Canadian government supplying nearly a quarter alongside Nvidia, Schwarz Group and BCE. US allies are now buying direct equity in national model makers to reduce reliance on American labs. Bloomberg / The Wall Street Journal / Semafor
  • The AI industry's alliance with the White House risks unraveling as public backlash grows before the midterms, with Meta's filings for a record $98 billion share sale now flagging possible new taxes, regulation and "shifting public sentiment." Policy support for the build-out can no longer be assumed beyond the current administration. Bloomberg
  • Shanghai's Iluvatar CoreX says its Tiangai 300 GPU matched and in multimodal tasks outperformed Nvidia's H200, the most advanced Nvidia chip approved for export to China, in a live demonstration. Each credible domestic alternative weakens the hold US export rules have over Chinese AI developers. South China Morning Post

Regulation & Trust

  • CrowdStrike says Chinese state-linked hackers used DeepSeek and other AI tools to target more than 200 organizations in 11 days, while researchers at Irregular found Z.AI's open-weight GLM-5.3 completed 3 of 13 multi-stage attack scenarios and refused none. Freely downloadable models are lowering the skill and staffing needed for state-scale intrusion. NBC News / Reuters
  • An Accenture employee's personal Claude agent ran on a Defense Department network for two months before it was identified and the vulnerability patched, sources told WIRED. Unsanctioned agents inside contractor environments are becoming a supply chain risk for government AI programs. WIRED
  • A poll of about 500 voters each in Ohio and Iowa found ten AI safety proposals, including government safety testing and bans on chatbots for minors, each drawing more than 70% support, even at some cost to US competitiveness. Demand for safety rules is now bipartisan in swing states. Semafor

  • Alphabet fell 13% for its worst day since its 2004 listing, erasing about $500 billion in value after the third-quarter cash burn covered in Thursday's brief. The rout dragged the Nasdaq lower and spread fears that the cost of the global build-out is outrunning its returns. CNBC / The Wall Street Journal

  • Credit markets are nearing their limit on AI debt, with yields on some data center bonds and loans in double digits, while HSBC is asking insurers to take credit risk on data center loans and Nvidia is discussing guarantees for an Nscale campus Anthropic plans to use. Suppliers and banks are increasingly underwriting demand themselves. Bloomberg / Bloomberg / Bloomberg

Incumbent software vendors must now show AI revenue arriving faster than disruption. CNBC
  • Samsung posted its fourth straight record quarter, with operating profit above 100 trillion won ($74.52 billion), and said tight memory supply will persist for years. Memory is now a binding constraint on AI capacity and a growing share of build-out costs. The Wall Street Journal

AI & Innovation

  • Mistral released open weights for Large 4, a model of about 1.05 trillion parameters aimed at cyber defense, with a modified MIT license and a $2.06 cost per benchmark task, 63% below the preview. It now leads open models built outside China but still trails DeepSeek and GLM on overall capability. Reuters / TechCrunch / VentureBeat
  • OpenAI rebuilt its Model Spec around five values led by faithfulness to human intent, noting that current models do not yet fully reflect it. Published behavior frameworks are becoming a baseline expectation for frontier labs. Reuters
  • Google is winding down Jules, its coding agent launched in December 2024, and folding its remote background-task features into the Antigravity platform. Coding agents are consolidating into single, agent-first development environments. The Verge

Strategic Counter Narrative

Canada's decision on October 29 to fund nearly a quarter of Cohere's $5 billion round shows governments treating model makers as strategic assets at the moment private capital starts charging more for AI risk. Mistral made the same case from Paris, pitching open weights as access that no outside provider can cut off. The Gulf entered this phase early: the IMF puts AI investment plans announced last year by the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar above 5% of GDP, about $120 billion, and Oracle reports that data sovereignty now leads most regional customer conversations. Abu Dhabi holds sovereign demand and patient capital in the same place, an edge that grows as Amazon and Alphabet show how expensive the build-out becomes when cash runs short. The ADNOC, XRG and Microsoft study adds a second signal from the energy sector, where agentic workflow use more than doubled in a year while markets still debate returns.

Watch Outs

  • Voters in 10 US states will decide on November 3 whether to curb data center development, with 41 local measures in 38 jurisdictions and 21 proposals in Ohio alone; should restrictive measures pass, US capacity timelines would slip further. South China Morning Post
  • US GDP grew at a 3.4% annualized rate in the third quarter on AI investment, and economists said the data strengthen the case for a Federal Reserve rate rise in December; should the Fed hike, the cost of debt-funded data center projects would rise further. Reuters
