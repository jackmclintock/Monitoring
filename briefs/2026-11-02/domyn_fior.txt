Top Line

As private investors retreat from AI, states and national champions are stepping in to fund it. Aramco has closed its $2.6 billion purchase of 49% of Humain, and South Korea plans to nearly double public spending on chips, physical AI and data centers in a record 2027 budget. Markets are moving the other way, with foreign investors pulling a record sum from Taiwanese stocks in October and SK Hynix shelving a US listing for its flash-memory unit. Politics is shifting as well, as OpenAI readies a post-midterm push for mandatory safeguards on the most powerful AI systems while DeepSeek releases a coding agent built to need less human supervision. Abu Dhabi opens ADIPEC with AI in energy on the agenda, after Sheikh Khaled met more than 100 global leaders at the ENACT Majlis, and Dubai has announced Nad Zabeel, a research and AI campus for 30,000 innovators a year.

Strategic Counter Narrative

On November 1, Aramco completed its purchase of 49% of Humain, paying about $2.6 billion in cash and transferring its own AI assets into a company that aims to deploy 3 GW of data center capacity by 2030. The deal closed as private capital stepped back from AI, with record October foreign selling in Taiwan and SK Hynix pulling a planned US listing. Governments are filling part of that space, as South Korea proposes 21.3 trillion won for chips, physical AI and data centers and OpenAI prepares to argue for mandatory limits on the most powerful systems. Operators are also starting to show measured returns, with HD Hyundai Robotics reporting that AI agents cut problem resolution at its industrial sites from 21.5 days to 7.9 days. In the Gulf, the emphasis is turning to where AI is applied, from energy efficiency and system resilience at the ENACT Majlis to an agentic overhaul of Dubai’s transport authority. ADIPEC opens in Abu Dhabi with energy companies weighing how quickly AI can raise output and resilience without adding new risks to critical systems.
