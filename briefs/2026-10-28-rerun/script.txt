Good morning and here's today's news for Wednesday, the twenty-eighth of October twenty twenty-six.

The largest buyers of AI compute are spreading their bets beyond Nvidia, and the money to pay for it is arriving from the suppliers themselves. Oracle used its AI World conference to put Google's TPUs, Cerebras and SambaNova chips alongside multi-gigawatt Nvidia clusters, while OpenAI is reported to be moving from renting Google chips to buying them outright. Meta priced an upsized twenty-five billion dollar share sale on five times demand, with Nvidia taking three billion of it as the two deepen work on the Muse agent, the same vendor-finances-customer pattern as the Broadcom and Apollo deals earlier in the week. Governments are turning that spending into policy, with the Netherlands cutting twenty-five thousand civil service jobs on the promise of AI productivity, and Anthropic asking California to let courts suspend dangerous models. Banks moved the other way on agents, with R B C blocking third-party assistants from customer accounts.

Turning to the compute market. At Oracle AI World, Oracle said it will become the first cloud outside Google to offer Google's TPUs from twenty twenty-seven, alongside Cerebras and SambaNova chips and OpenAI's own accelerator built with Broadcom, and its shares rose nearly five percent. Oracle did not say how the new capacity will be paid for, and its investor day later this week is where that question gets answered. OpenAI, meanwhile, is lining up a purchase of Google TPUs to add capacity and diversify suppliers, with final terms not yet set. Meta's share sale cleared at six hundred and twenty-five dollars a share, with the fifteen largest orders taking about seventy percent, and Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom all indicating interest in buying stock in their largest customer. Further down the hardware chain, Infineon will invest about one point one billion euros in Austria to mass-produce gallium nitride power chips for AI data centers, because power delivery is now one of the hard limits on gigawatt campuses.

On the regulation front. Anthropic has written to Governor Newsom proposing that embedded evaluators report risks to California's attorney general, who could then ask a court to suspend a model. OpenAI's letter a week earlier backed shutdown controls and auditors but stopped short of court-ordered blocks, so the two largest labs are now competing to write California's rulebook, and the legislature returns in about a month. In Canada, Royal Bank of Canada will block third-party AI agents such as Meta's Muse from its online banking after a security review, and Bank of America has told customers they are responsible for anything an agent does with their credentials. And in Britain, the immigration inspector is considering a review of Home Office asylum tools built on OpenAI models, after checks found twenty-two percent of case summaries inaccurate or incomplete.

On the product side, Google DeepMind released Genie 4, a real-time world model with native audio and scenes that stay consistent for up to two minutes, with Gemini acting as a director in a robotics experiment. Liquid AI released six small open-weight models that run browser agents, vision and speech on a device in about one gigabyte, with Mercedes-Benz and Shopify as early testers. And Anthropic published drug development results with six partners, including an eighty-nine percent cut in regulatory review time at one of them.

And coverage of Al-Futtaim's study on AI in the GCC workforce continued, with Inception42's chief operating officer Ibrahim Al Jallaf quoted on companies that drop AI into workflows designed for people, then wonder why the gains are marginal.
