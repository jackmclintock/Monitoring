## Strategic Counter Narrative
Oracle's decision at AI World on October 27 to sell Google's TPUs, Cerebras and SambaNova chips beside multi-gigawatt Nvidia clusters is the clearest statement yet that the next phase of the buildout will be multi-silicon, and OpenAI's reported move to buy TPUs outright confirms that the largest model builders want more than one supplier. The capital behind that shift increasingly comes from inside the industry, with Nvidia putting $3 billion into Meta's share sale on the same day, which means the buyers and sellers of compute are financing each other. In the Gulf, the two largest alternative asset managers in the world made the same bet in different words, with Marc Rowan citing "unbelievable demand for compute" to explain Apollo's stake in Mubadala Capital and Stephen Schwarzman calling Abu Dhabi the most progressive place in AI as Blackstone plans to triple or quadruple its Gulf assets. Capital of that scale raises the question of what it buys once the chips are installed. For Inception42, Ibrahim Al Jallaf's point in the Al-Futtaim coverage, that companies drop AI into workflows designed for people, is the practical counterpart to it: the compute will arrive, and the returns will depend on redesigning the work around the agents.
