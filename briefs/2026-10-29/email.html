Good morning team,

Top Line

Investors have started pricing AI companies on the cash their spending returns, and the earnings season is now sorting winners from laggards on that basis. Microsoft beat expectations with 48% Azure growth and spent less than forecast, a sign that partner data center capacity is arriving more slowly, while Oracle's shares jumped after it set a date for returning to positive free cash flow. Alphabet went the other way, falling more than 10% after hours on a search revenue miss and a second straight quarter of negative free cash flow, even as its cloud unit beat estimates. Debt markets remain open but more selective, with global bond sales at a record while banks delay a sale tied to an Anthropic data center in Texas. Google's decision to release Gemini 4 Carbon first to cybersecurity defenders shows that frontier labs now treat who gets a model first as part of the launch itself.

Power

  • China's draft AI export rules would add 23 technologies to its restricted list, including advanced models, autonomous agents and chip design tools, allowing foreign users to access Chinese AI through cloud services while requiring approval for transfers of the underlying technology. Beijing is formalizing a model of keeping intellectual property at home while selling access abroad, with adoption expected by year-end. Reuters
  • Britain will give its AI Security Institute £120 million (about $160 million) in new funding, more than ten times the annual budget of the US testing center CAISSI, which has had no permanent director since July. Pre-release model testing capacity is shifting toward London, after the White House asked OpenAI and Anthropic to hold new models from UK testers until US reviewers had seen them. Semafor
  • Malaysian customs seized about $12.1 million of advanced AI chips hidden in 30 servers falsely declared as electronic components at Kuala Lumpur airport, linking the shipment to a syndicate tied to at least five cases worth more than 100 million ringgit. Transit hubs are now enforcing US-origin chip controls directly, raising compliance scrutiny on every regional route for advanced hardware. Reuters

Regulation & Trust

  • New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law banning a dozen "unsafe AI companion features" when chatbots interact with users under 18, including pretending to be human, with age verification required and penalties that can include shutting a chatbot down. With Congress inactive, New York and California are setting the de facto US rules for consumer AI. U.S. News / The Verge
  • The US Justice Department is questioning Nvidia about its nearly $13 billion acquisition of Hugging Face, the open model platform used by more than 18 million people, and how the deal may affect competitors. Antitrust scrutiny is moving from chips to the open software layer, where Nvidia's $20 billion Groq asset deal has also drawn DOJ scrutiny. CNBC
  • Security researchers say the PHP flaws that AI agents uncovered earlier in October have drawn millions of hacking attempts but few successful attacks, as the vulnerabilities proved hard to exploit and companies patched quickly. Executives warn the volume of AI-discovered bugs will shift corporate security toward resilience, meaning fast detection, containment and recovery. Axios

Enterprise & Investment

  • Microsoft's revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion, with Azure up 48% to $33.2 billion against 44% expected, while capital spending of $45.2 billion came in below the $50.7 billion estimate, a gap Microsoft put down to payment timing and fewer finance leases. CoreWeave and Nebius fell almost 5% after hours on signs that partner capacity is arriving more slowly. Bloomberg / CNBC / TechCrunch
  • Microsoft also disclosed $10 billion of OpenAI-related revenue in the quarter, nearly 11% of the total, as its stake in OpenAI slipped to about 24% from about 25% in June. OpenAI has become one of Microsoft's largest single revenue sources even as its ownership is gradually diluted. TechCrunch
  • Alphabet shares fell more than 10% after hours as search revenue of $63.4 billion missed estimates and free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion, though Google Cloud sales of $30.7 billion beat forecasts. Investors are now penalizing heavy AI spending when the core business that funds it slows. The National
  • Following up on the Watch Out in Wednesday's brief, Oracle told its investor day it will return to positive free cash flow in the second half of fiscal 2028, reach more than $30 billion in fiscal 2029 and cap new capital raises through fiscal 2030 at $20 billion. The shares rose about 11%, showing that a credible funding plan now matters as much as compute supply. Bloomberg / Oracle
  • Global bond sales reached a record $6.61 trillion this year on AI borrowing, yet banks are delaying the sale of part of a $15 billion loan for an Anthropic-linked Texas data center after the state paused new projects to assess grid risks. Capital is still available, but power grid approvals are now setting the pace of AI financing. Bloomberg / Bloomberg
  • Anthropic's IPO filing shows an 85% gross margin on running its existing models, excluding training costs, against a headline net loss of about $80 billion. The figure suggests pay-as-you-go AI can be profitable at the serving layer, with training costs left as the main drain on profits. Semafor

AI & Innovation

  • Google launched Gemini 4 Carbon, which tops the Artificial Analysis index at 59 ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, with stronger vulnerability discovery and patching, available first only to selected defenders in its Fairwind Program. Google is making staged access part of the release for models with offensive cyber capability. Reuters / VentureBeat
  • Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, a free open-weight model that transcribes up to 40 languages, translates 31 to and from English and separates up to eight speakers on a single chip. Open speech models lower the cost of voice agents, especially for multilingual markets. TechCrunch
  • Anthropic launched reporting tools that show enterprise customers what teams produced with Claude, what it cost and where workflows broke down, alongside pooled group budgets. Enterprise AI sales are moving from usage volume to measurable output per token. TechCrunch

Alliances and Partnerships

  • Shield AI signed an MoU at Milipol Qatar with Rheinmetall Barzan Advanced Technologies, the Barzan Holdings and Rheinmetall joint venture, to explore integrating its Hivemind autonomy software into Qatari unmanned surface vessels. Gulf defense groups are pairing local manufacturing with US autonomy software to build sovereign capability in maritime AI. Zawya

Abu Dhabi & Gulf

  • Canada's Terrestar signed an MoU in Ottawa, during Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed's visit, to build open satellite standards with Equatys, the venture Space42 and Viasat are forming with up to $1 billion in combined equity. The pact widens a UAE-led direct-to-device alternative to Starlink, targeting a first system by 2029. Quartz
  • Oracle, AstraZeneca and M42 are using AI to link health records with clinical trial systems in Abu Dhabi, starting with hypertension, as AstraZeneca invests $50 million in trials with Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi. M42's Ossi platform, built with Microsoft, is expected to give doctors up to 60% more time with patients across seven centers. The National
  • Dubai Future Foundation opened an AI sandbox with the Dubai Centre for Artificial Intelligence where AI-native companies can test products with regulators and possibly obtain exemptions, with applications closing 18 December. Dubai is building a regulatory route from pilot to deployment for companies stuck at the experimentation stage. Zawya
  • S&P Global puts the average cost of building data centers at $11 million per megawatt in the UAE, against $13 million in Saudi Arabia and North America, as shipping disruption could raise regional build costs by 10% this year. Delivered cost per megawatt is becoming a key measure of competition between Gulf compute hubs. AGBI

Strategic Counter Narrative

Microsoft's capital spending in the quarter to September came in about $5.5 billion below forecasts at $45.2 billion, and the shares of the neoclouds that build its capacity fell on signs that new capacity is arriving more slowly. The binding limits on AI are shifting to construction, power and grid approvals, as Texas's pause on new data center projects and the delayed bond sale for an Anthropic-linked site show. Investors are rewarding companies that turn spending into revenue, such as Microsoft and Oracle, and marking down those whose core business slows while spending rises. The UAE competes well on this measure, with build costs of about $11 million per megawatt against $13 million in Saudi Arabia and North America, while Khazna develops the 1-gigawatt Stargate UAE cluster. Abu Dhabi will host Data Center World's first Middle East edition on November 17 and 18, a sign of how far the region's buildout now draws the global infrastructure industry. Inception42 faces a similar test as enterprise buyers start asking, as Anthropic's new reporting tools do, what each token of AI spending actually delivers.

WSJ / Bloomberg
