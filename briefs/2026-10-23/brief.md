## Top Line
With public markets turning against AI spending, the sector’s largest bets are now closing on sovereign and state-aligned capital, and that capital is setting the price. OpenAI is nearing a raise of up to $30 billion led by UAE investors and Coatue at a lower valuation than it first sought, with a separate deal for more UAE computing capacity under negotiation. DeepSeek’s roughly $15 billion round, drawn largely from Chinese industrial groups, landed alongside Beijing clearing it and MiniMax to deploy abroad, giving China’s leading labs both the funding and the permission to expand overseas. Listed markets are less forgiving, with SK Hynix shelving Solidigm’s US listing after a chip selloff, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moving to dismiss systemic risk, and Barclays pairing a profit miss with plans to cut 5,000 roles. Abu Dhabi’s €12.75 billion in agreements with Greece, with AI and digital infrastructure prominent among them, shows the same capital working outward from the Gulf.

## Strategic Counter Narrative
US banks have found that they cannot reliably tell whether an AI agent inside a customer account was sent by that customer or is acting on its own, and are cutting agents back to read-only access. The same problem will follow agents into every regulated sector, because the constraint on deployment is now proof of who authorized an action and what an agent is allowed to do, and stronger models do not supply that proof. Barclays’ chief executive pointed the same way in describing AI as the icing on years of infrastructure work, a sequence that rewards organizations building identity, permissions and audit into their systems before agents arrive. Abu Dhabi is attracting enterprise vendors on that basis, with Databricks moving its regional team to the capital, closer to government and public-sector clients, in a market where the UAE has built its computing capacity and the rules for using it side by side. For Inception42, the banks’ predicament sets the standard its agentic deployments in the region are built to meet: each agent carries a verifiable mandate, with permissions and logs held where it cannot alter them.
