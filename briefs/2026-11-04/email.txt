Top Line

Two juries sat on the AI build-out on Tuesday: the credit markets that fund it and the voters who host it. AMD's near-doubling of data center revenue lifted chip stocks after three days of losses, even as Meta returned to bond investors for up to $25 billion and Apollo booked a record $114 billion of new lending on AI demand. At the ballot box, a pro-data center Republican won Florida's governorship by a far thinner margin than his predecessor, while Texas, with more than a quarter of US data center capacity, chose a governor after a September halt on new permits. Agent security became a market in its own right, with CrowdStrike paying $495 million for two startups and Apple warning that agents may act without authorization. In Abu Dhabi, ADNOC said it now runs more than 200 AI applications, and Sam Altman told ADIPEC that the UAE is the world's most forward-looking country for AI adoption.

Strategic Counter Narrative

Apple's new annual report warns that AI agents may take actions "not directed, instructed or authorized" and that its ability to oversee third-party agents may be limited. The industry's answer is arriving as products: CrowdStrike now sells a kill switch that cuts an agent off from its model, and Anthropic has published a way to prove which model produced an output, while China moves to write such controls into law. In the Gulf, the same principle is being spoken at the top of the energy industry. At ADIPEC, Sultan Al Jaber said systems do not carry responsibility, people do, and ADNOC Offshore's chief executive said people stay responsible for decisions as AI spreads across operations. The National's editorial on the OpenAI agreements asked that officials be able to question automated outputs, correct errors and explain decisions. At ADNOC, where Inception42 is deploying OpenAI's frontier models, human experts will oversee the major decisions. As ADIPEC runs to 5 November, energy companies from more than 170 countries are weighing how far to let AI into the systems they run.
