Top Line

Two juries sat on the AI build-out on Tuesday: the credit markets that fund it and the voters who host it. AMD's near-doubling of data center revenue lifted chip stocks after three days of losses, even as Meta returned to bond investors for up to $25 billion and Apollo booked a record $114 billion of new lending on AI demand. At the ballot box, a pro-data center Republican won Florida's governorship by a far thinner margin than his predecessor, while Texas, with more than a quarter of US data center capacity, chose a governor after a September halt on new permits. Agent security became a market in its own right, with CrowdStrike paying $495 million for two startups and Apple warning that agents may act without authorization. In Abu Dhabi, ADNOC said it now runs more than 200 AI applications, and Sam Altman told ADIPEC that the UAE is the world's most forward-looking country for AI adoption.

Power

  • Nvidia told its cloud computing clients to make sure at least 51% of their services go to companies outside China, the Financial Times reported. The chipmaker is now writing Washington's China policy into its own commercial terms, turning neocloud customer books into a front line of export control. Financial Times / Bloomberg
  • China's National Development and Reform Commission said it is speeding up legislation to govern AI across the full model life cycle, from data to computing power, covering developers such as DeepSeek, Moonshot and Alibaba. Beijing is setting binding loss-of-control rules at a time when Western safety commitments remain largely voluntary. Bloomberg
  • Britain's NCSC, the national cyber agency, said the UK is likely to face AI-enabled attacks on critical infrastructure by 2028, with China and Russia expected to use AI routinely for geopolitical aims. The assessment puts electricity switching, water controls and factory lines on the list of systems that AI could expose at scale. Financial Times / Qatar Tribune
  • Republican Byron Donalds, who backed data center growth, won Florida's governorship with just over 50% of the vote, a far tighter margin than Ron DeSantis in 2022. In Texas, where Greg Abbott halted data center permitting in September, more than 80% of voters told pollsters they worry about the facilities' effect on water and power bills. The Verge / CNBC

Regulation & Trust

  • Apple's annual filing lists AI agents as a risk, warning they may "behave in unintended or unpredictable ways," including taking actions not authorized, and that its ability to control third-party agents may be limited. A company with no agentic product of its own is already pricing agent liability into its disclosures. CNBC
  • EU Court of Justice Advocate General Maciej Szpunar said a chatbot that retrieves and reuses press articles to answer users may infringe publishers' rights, in a case brought against Google over Gemini, while training on such material falls under the data mining exception. Retrieval, rather than training, is becoming the copyright exposure for AI search in Europe. Reuters / Wall Street Journal
  • Following up Monday's brief, Binance's post-mortem says North Korea's TraderTraitor unit used fake job interviews to plant malware and then Binance's own internal AI tool to take control of wallets before the $6.3 billion theft. Internal AI tools with wallet access are now part of the attack surface. The Verge
  • Google will switch off Search entirely for K-12 student accounts whose schools decline its Gemini features, with no option to keep Search alone. The choice ties core search access to AI adoption in classrooms used by more than 150 million teachers and students. The Verge

Enterprise & Investment

  • AMD reported third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion against $13.1 billion expected, with data center sales nearly doubling to 64% of the total, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $17 billion against $16.2 billion forecast. Shares rose 8% after hours, as Lisa Su said she expects server revenue to double in 2027. Wall Street Journal / CNBC / Semafor
  • Meta began marketing bonds for the first time in six months, seeking $20 billion to $25 billion across dollar and euro tranches after a quarter of negative free cash flow and capex guidance of $135 billion to $145 billion. S&P had already moved its outlook to negative, so each new raise now tests rating headroom as well as demand. Bloomberg / CNBC
  • Apollo posted record quarterly originations of $114 billion, up 52% from a year earlier, with debt investments making up $98 billion, even as it reported a net loss. Private credit is becoming a primary lender to the AI build-out as public bond markets grow more selective. Wall Street Journal / Financial Times
  • CrowdStrike agreed to buy Fiddler AI for about $400 million and Dreadnode for about $95 million to monitor and test AI agents, and launched a kill switch that cuts rogue agents off from the models driving them. With Gartner sizing the AI security market near $5 billion in 2027, agent control is now an acquisition category. Wall Street Journal / TechCrunch / CNBC
  • Inference startup Fal doubled its valuation to $16 billion in a $1.3 billion round led by Bessemer, with Nvidia and T. Rowe Price joining, after demand forced it to rate-limit customers over the summer. Inference capacity, rather than model training, is now drawing late-stage capital. Bloomberg / TechCrunch

AI & Innovation

  • Anthropic published FlashProve, an open-source research system that would let labs publish a cryptographic fingerprint for each model so that any output can be verified as coming from it, without revealing weights. Provable inference would let customers confirm they are getting the model they pay for, though no lab plans to deploy it yet. TechCrunch
  • Apple opened Siri AI to third-party developers in an iOS 27.3 preview, letting it carry out tasks inside "all apps" using personal context and on-screen awareness, and added "app skills" that chain several actions into one request. Apple is building an agent layer through its own assistant while its filing warns about agents it does not control. The Verge

Strategic Counter Narrative

Apple's new annual report warns that AI agents may take actions "not directed, instructed or authorized" and that its ability to oversee third-party agents may be limited. The industry's answer is arriving as products: CrowdStrike now sells a kill switch that cuts an agent off from its model, and Anthropic has published a way to prove which model produced an output, while China moves to write such controls into law. In the Gulf, the same principle is being spoken at the top of the energy industry. At ADIPEC, Sultan Al Jaber said systems do not carry responsibility, people do, and ADNOC Offshore's chief executive said people stay responsible for decisions as AI spreads across operations. The National's editorial on the OpenAI agreements asked that officials be able to question automated outputs, correct errors and explain decisions. At ADNOC, where Inception42 is deploying OpenAI's frontier models, human experts will oversee the major decisions. As ADIPEC runs to 5 November, energy companies from more than 170 countries are weighing how far to let AI into the systems they run.
