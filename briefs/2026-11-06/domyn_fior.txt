Top Line

Two days after the midterms, investors turned the vote against data centers into a price. The Nasdaq fell 5.5% on Thursday, its deepest one-day drop since April 2025, the Nasdaq 100 lost $2.5 trillion in value and the selling spread overnight to chipmakers in Seoul, Tokyo and Taipei. Washington supplied the reasons, with Senator Bernie Sanders calling for a national data center moratorium and new governors in states holding about 40% of planned US capacity promising to pause construction. Capital is already pulling back: potential investors are questioning whether Anthropic can hold its $1.5 trillion IPO valuation, and Microsoft has walked away from an option to more than double its Texas compute deal with Nscale. OpenAI answered with a community compact that pays for its own power and drops nondisclosure agreements with officials, an early sign that public consent is becoming a condition of building AI in America.

Strategic Counter Narrative

Meta president Dina Powell McCormick said after the midterms that every hyperscaler is now asking whether it needs to build outside the United States, as governors-elect in Texas, Ohio and Georgia promise to pause new data centers. Capital is following that question: Google's $17 billion Wyoming commitment shows companies still build where governments welcome them, and investors are marking down the chip and neocloud companies most exposed to local opposition. For the Gulf, the advantage is alignment between state, energy and public opinion, and the UAE shows it, with 85% of workers already using AI at work and Dubai wiring every traffic signal to an AI control center. The same survey shows the gap to close, with two-thirds of UAE workers worried about their jobs and few employers explaining their plans. Inception42's Ashish Koshy made that case at ADIPEC, arguing for AI that augments workers. ADIPEC closed on Thursday with energy security and the use of AI in oil and gas as its two main themes.
