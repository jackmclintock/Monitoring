## Top Line
AI agents are becoming cheap enough to run everywhere, which moves the hard questions from building capacity to controlling how it is used. Anthropic's Haiku 5.5 cuts the cost of high-volume agent work by about 75 percent, and Microsoft's new Nvidia-powered Windows PCs run agents on the device inside Execution Containers that limit what they can touch. Frontier capacity remains a capital race, with SpaceX reportedly seeking $40 billion in debt to buy Nvidia chips, yet it is the cheaper systems built on that capacity that reach users at scale. In Washington, that spread is shaping the oversight agenda, from Senator Cantwell's proposal for mandatory risk disclosure to a nonprofit's unacceptable-risk rating for ChatGPT for Teens. Abu Dhabi is answering the same question through people and practice, with AiNative training the government workforce and the public, and AI Everything devoting its second day to safe deployment in public services, manufacturing and education.

## Regulation & Trust
- Senator Maria Cantwell has proposed mandatory AI risk disclosure, a framework that would require companies to report model risks in a standardized way. Such a rule would turn voluntary safety commitments into reporting obligations that boards and investors can compare across labs. The Hill
- A nonprofit assessment has classified OpenAI's ChatGPT for Teens as an unacceptable risk, bringing youth safety to the front of the debate on consumer AI products. Child protection is likely to be the first area where product design meets binding requirements. WSJ
- Americans are expressing serious anxiety about AI's impact, a sentiment that shapes the political appetite for proposals such as mandatory disclosure. Public opinion of this kind tends to build legislative momentum before technical consensus forms. CBS Atlanta
